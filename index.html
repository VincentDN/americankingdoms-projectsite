What if the Americas had been settled during the Dark Ages?

In American Kingdoms, American history is re-imagined as an Arthurian medieval epic – an age defined not by musket and cannon, but by knightly honor and courtly intrigue. This alt-history project is a collaborative worldbuilding effort: anyone can join our wiki to add their own character or fief to the strange but familiar setting of American Kingdoms. Join a medieval pastiche where the Natives fight on equal footing with conquistadores, and where Asian, European and American cultures clash for control of the New World.

“Thirteen hundred, four score and seven years ago, on this continent, a covenant was struck between a free people and their God.”

The year is 1377. After years of unrest, the Outremer Kingdoms of the eastern coast rise against their British overlords, igniting a rebellion that will engulf a continent.

As the perfidious Redcloaks are driven from American cities, thirteen sovereign dukes forge an audacious new order: a Union of equals, bound by charter and crowned as the Union of American Kingdoms. Its High King will reign not by birthright, but by the consent of his peers, elected from among them to defend the ancient liberties of the realm.

Part feudal compact, part republican experiment, this fragile Union promises freedom from the Old World. It may yet tear the New World apart.

The American Revolutionary War has begun.

The Union

The Thirteen Realms bound as a Holy American Empire, its High King elected and raised from among his peers. Strong in ideal, fragile in practice, and the prize every ambitious lord covets.

The Natives

The first nations of the continent, who meet the newcomers as equals in war and in council. Proud and unbroken, they defend ancestral lands against every crown that would claim them.

Read the Wiki article

The Redcloaks

The British crown and its loyalists, driven from the continent twenty years ago. The perfidious Redcloaks have not forgotten the New World, and scheme to bring the American Kings to heel once more.

Read the Wiki article

The Southern Kingdoms

Proud chivalric realms of the warm South, where courtly honour, the tournament and the bloodline rule as surely as any law. Fierce keepers of tradition and the old codes.

Read the Wiki article

Vinland

Norse-blooded settlers of the frozen north, raiders and seafarers whose claim to the New World runs deeper than any crown's. Bound to the sea, the saga and the old gods.

Read the Wiki article

The Free Cities

As settlers spread inland, free cities and trade republics took root far beyond the reach of both the Redcloaks and the Union. Ruled by councils of coin, they answer only to profit and their own hard-won liberty.

Read the Wiki article

Dragon Coast

Along the western shores rises the Dragon Coast: colonies of Ming-descended settlers who crossed the great ocean generations ago, bringing the steel, silk and statecraft of the Middle Kingdom to a new frontier.

Read the Wiki article

The Roman Settlers

Roman mariners reached America around the year 0, founding distant colonies and leaving traces of their language, craft and law among the Native peoples. Their settlements vanished centuries ago; today, Rome’s American heirs survive only in disputed ruins, tribal memory and legend.

The Sidennic League

Across Antillia, the Seven Sister Cities preserve their Roman inheritance through a council-led league of merchant republics. Bound together since 555, their patricians, condottieri and traders have turned isolation into a nine-century experiment in commerce, rivalry and survival. Read on the Wiki

The Pueblo Kingdom

Along the Rio Grande, fortified mesa cities and terrace farms form a sovereign league under Tu’utawa and its Sun King. Roman contact shaped its engineering and trade, yet Pueblo institutions, waterworks and dynastic legends remain distinctly their own. Read on the Wiki

New France

Centered on New Orleans and the Mississippi, this Franco-Creole kingdom rose among Roman ruins and blended African, Native and European traditions. The Council of Ashes ignited its rebellion, while Papal and Union aid helped House Lafayette secure independence. Read on the Wiki

The Shining Republic of Mahatamus

A thousand-year republic across Texas and northern Mexico, Mahatamus joins Roman political forms with Coahuiltecan culture. Its elected Iacanor, three-part Senate, competitive merchant clans and formidable legions make it a great power of the Gulf. Read on the Wiki

“From the Roman settlers to the Thirteen Kingdoms... discover the world's largest exhibition on the 1375 Revolutionary war, and the American Medieval Age! Manhattan Castle at Old Town, New York is proud to host a year-round exhibition exploring this defining chapter in American history.”

Originally developed in 1959 by renowned illustrator L.C. Meyenbecker and commissioned by the Royal Galleries of New York, the American Medieval Exhibition poster series has become synonymous with the era it depicts. Meyenbecker’s defining image - a knight of the Thirteen Kingdoms draped in the American banner, his squire presenting the sword to his outstretched hand - endures as one of the most powerful visual icons of medieval America.

American Kingdoms is a cross-media project by the Kaiser Cat Collective (known on YouTube as Kaiser Cat Cinema). KCC is a group of alt-history content creators, born from the modding scene of the early 2000s and united by a passion for the alt-history genre. Since our founding in 2016, we've built alt-history worlds through crowdfunding and cross-media content: music covers, animations, YouTube videos, and even a live-action short film set in the great "what ifs" of alt-history worldbuilding.
